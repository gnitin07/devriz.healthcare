You finally get a clear week. The pimples fade, your skin calms down, and you
start to feel like yourself again. Then one morning it is back — the same
cheek, the same jawline, the same story.

If that sounds familiar, you are not doing something wrong. Acne coming back is
not a sign that you failed. It is a sign that something underneath was never
addressed. Here are the five reasons we see most often in consultations.

## 1. You treated the pimple, not the cause

Most of what people use — spot creams, face washes, drying gels — works on the
pimple that is already on your face. None of it changes why that pimple formed.

Acne starts below the surface, weeks before you see anything. Oil production,
clogged pores, bacteria and inflammation all build up quietly. A spot treatment
handles the last step of that chain. The chain itself keeps running.

This is why clearing your face and stopping feels like winning, right up until
the next batch arrives.

## 2. You stopped as soon as it looked better

This is the single most common reason acne repeats.

Skin takes roughly four to six weeks to turn over a full layer of cells. A
routine that is genuinely working is still working underneath long after your
face looks calm. Stopping at the first clear week means stopping halfway.

The fix is unglamorous: keep going for the full course your consultant sets,
even during the stretch where nothing seems to be happening.

## 3. Your routine is too harsh

Scrubbing, drying, stripping, using three actives at once because each one had
good reviews — this is the pattern we see constantly, and it backfires.

When you strip your skin barrier, your skin responds by producing more oil to
compensate. More oil means more clogged pores. Harsh routines feel productive
because your face is squeaky clean afterwards, but they often make month three
worse than month one.

If your skin stings, flakes or feels tight after washing, your routine is too
aggressive for it.

## 4. It may be hormonal

Acne along the jawline, chin and lower cheeks — especially if it flares on a
monthly cycle — often has a hormonal driver behind it.

No face wash will fix that. It is not a hygiene problem and it is not
something you can scrub away. Hormonal acne needs to be recognised for what it
is, and the approach is different from the one that works for teenage acne or
oily-skin breakouts.

Getting this identified early saves months of using products that were never
going to work.

## 5. The product was never matched to your skin

A friend's routine cleared her skin, so you tried it. It did nothing, or made
things worse.

That is expected. Two people with visible acne can have completely different
skin types, different triggers and different sensitivities. A product built for
very oily skin will wreck dry, sensitive skin. A gentle routine will barely
register on skin producing a lot of oil.

Buying by review rather than by diagnosis is close to guessing.

## What actually breaks the cycle

The pattern that works looks like this:

- **Get the type identified first.** Hormonal, bacterial, product-triggered and
  stress-linked acne look similar and are treated differently.
- **Match the routine to your skin type**, not to what worked for someone else.
- **Give it the full course.** Real change shows over weeks, not days.
- **Keep it simple.** Two or three products used consistently beat seven used
  erratically.
- **Protect the barrier.** Gentle cleansing and daily sun protection do more
  long-term work than any single active ingredient.

## When it is worth talking to someone

If your acne has come back more than twice, if it is painful or leaving marks,
or if you have been through several products with no lasting result, guessing
again is unlikely to help.

A proper diagnosis tells you which of the five reasons above is actually yours.
That one piece of information is usually the difference between another six
months of trial and error and a routine that finally holds.
